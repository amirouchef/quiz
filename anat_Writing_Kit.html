Anatomy writing kit

Topic 3 (bones, fossae, cavities) and Topic 5 (muscles) · for open questions in English

1 · The two frames the slides already use

Muscle (Topic 5): shape, path, function, innervation
Shape — The [muscle] is a [thin / thick / wide…] muscle. Path — It runs from [origin] to [insertion]. Function — It [elevates / retracts…] the [structure]. Innervation — It is innervated by [nerve].

Example: The masseter is a short, thick and rectangular muscle. It runs from the zygomatic arch to the lateral side of the angle and ramus of the mandible. It elevates the mandible. It is innervated by the mandibular nerve (V3).

Bone (Topic 3): location, shape, articulations, contents, parts
Location — The [bone] is located in [area of the skull]. Shape — It is a [flat / irregular / paired] bone. Articulations — It articulates with [bones]. Contents — It contains [sinus / foramen]. Parts — It is formed by [number] parts: …

Example: The sphenoid is located in the middle portion of the skull base, between the ethmoid and the basilar part of the occipital bone. It is shaped like a butterfly. It articulates with the frontal, ethmoid, occipital, parietal, temporal, palatine and zygomatic bones. It contains the sphenoid sinus. It is formed by four parts: the body, the greater wings, the lesser wings and the pterygoid process.

2 · Sentence templates

Foramen or canal
The [foramen] is located in the [bone / portion]. It transmits the [nerve / vessels]. e.g. the stylomastoid foramen transmits the facial nerve.
Wall, floor or roof
The [roof / floor / medial wall / lateral wall] is formed by [bones], with a small contribution from [bone].
Nerve
The [nerve] is a branch of [nerve / trunk]. It supplies [muscle] and gives sensory innervation to [structure].
List
There are [number] [items]: first, … ; second, … ; and finally, …
Compare
Both [A] and [B] …, but [A] …, whereas [B] …
Position
It lies between [X] and [Y] / It wraps [structure] / It forms [ligament].

5 · Nerves: write them in full once

  • V3 = mandibular nerve (trigeminal, CN5): masticatory muscles
  • CN7 = facial nerve: muscles of mimicry
  • CN9 = glossopharyngeal · CN10 = vagus
  • CN12 = hypoglossal nerve: tongue muscles
  • Pharyngeal plexus = CN9 + CN10

Write “mandibular nerve (V3)” the first time, then V3 is fine.

7 · In the exam

  • Count the key points. “Describe the X” usually means four points: write four short sentences. Name every item of a list and give the number first (“There are three fascias: …”).
  • Short and correct beats long and risky. Blank on a detail? Write what you know in order and move on: partial answers earn points.
  • Check your spelling against section 6 before handing in. Ask the professor if a list or bullet answer is accepted.
