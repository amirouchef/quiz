Motor branches of the mandibular nerve (V3)

Of the three branches of the trigeminal nerve, only V3 has motor fibers. Two groups: masticatory (masseter, temporalis, medial and lateral pterygoid) and associated or suprahyoid (mylohyoid, geniohyoid, anterior belly of the digastric).

Fascias of the masticatory muscles

Fascias are aponeuroses that wrap the muscles, made of layers of dense non-oriented connective tissue.

FasciaExtentFeatures
Temporal fasciaFrom the superior temporal line to the zygomatic archTwo layers inferiorly, including adipose tissue; separated from the muscle by adipose tissue that increases from top to bottom
Masseteric fasciaFrom the zygomatic arch to the posterior and inferior borders of the mandible and the anterior edge of the coronoid processThinner; wraps the anterior portion of the parotid gland and the parotid duct; adipose layer increasing from top to bottom
Interpterygoid fasciaBetween the medial and lateral pterygoid musclesThicker and more resistant posteriorly, forming the sphenomandibular ligament

Inconsistencies in the slides

  • Geniohyoid: listed with the trigeminal (V3) group, but its own slide gives C1 via the hypoglossal nerve (XII). Mylohyoid and geniohyoid have identical function text.
  • Palatoglossus: part 1 says it retracts the tongue to the palate; the deglutition slides say it descends the soft palate and elevates the tongue.
  • Glossopharyngeal portion of the superior constrictor: part 1 gives the hypoglossal nerve; the deglutition slides give the pharyngeal plexus for the constrictors.
  • Genioglossus and hyoglossus are described as retracting the tongue; ask your professor which wording is expected.
  • Vertical muscle of the tongue: its path is written as the same as the longitudinal muscle (hyoid minor horns).
